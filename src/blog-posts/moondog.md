>>> “I’m not gonna die in 4/4 time”

Once a blind man with a long flowing beard stood on the corner of
6 th Avenue, New York, between 52 nd and 55 th St. In the nights he
would sleep in doorways. By day he busked with peculiar
instruments of his own invention and sold poetry for 25cents a
piece, dressed head-to-toe as a Norse God. But mostly he just
stood there, silently holding his spear, like an apparition. He
became locally known as the Viking of 6 th avenue, even featured
as a tourist attraction in 1960s tourist guidebooks, but few were
aware of his secret life as a classical composer.

Louis Thomas Hardin aka Moondog was born a slither over a century ago
on May 26 th 1916. Blinded when a cap of dynamite exploded in his face
at the age of 16, he adopted his pseudonym in honour of a childhood

Naz Malik
pet that used to howl at the moon. The son of protestant missionary
parents, a young Moondog regularly visited American Indian
reservations and became inspired by their songs. Later he would revisit
Native American musical culture during a trip west in 1948, taking part in
the ceremonial music of the Navaho and Blackfoot tribes.
“When I was about 6 my dad, who was a missionary, went to a
convention at the Arapaho Reservation in Wyoming and at that time
they were doing the Sun Dance. Chief Yellow Cap let me sit on his lap
and he gave me a drumstick and let me beat on the tom-tom. And that&#39;s
how it started. I learned the running beat, which is bom-bom-bom-bom,
and the walking beat, bom-bom-bom. And I used those rhythms on my
newest record […] while I was playing a big bass drum.&quot;

This organic approach to sound become a key part of his compositional
architecture. In 1949 he moved to New York City and began living on the
streets and busking in the doorways of 6th Avenue, teaching himself the
art of composition through a lifetime of listening, soaking up the sounds
that surrounded him. He was inspired by the hustle and the bustle, the
chaos and the dirt of unremarkable life. Whilst jazz, gospel, blues and
boogie-woogie were plugging in and soaring out in all directions,
Moondog stayed so close to earth he sounded like the sidewalk, freezing
a forgotten history of mid-century New York City in music.

Over the years he developed his “snaketime”, a slithery rhythm
travelling in unusual time signatures. He crafted his own instruments

Naz Malik
such as Trimbas (triangular drums), the Hüs (a small stringed instrument,
played with a bow) and the Oo (a pointed harp with piano strings),
created for performing whilst squatting in doorways. Using these
unorthodox tools, Moondog found a way to compose rich and distinctive
music. British documentary maker Holly Elson, currently producing
Moondog’s biopic, The Viking of 6 th Avenue, describes his writing
process:
“He wouldn&#39;t compose at a piano, but while standing on the street. He
would have a Braille slate with a puncher and punch the notation into
the card under his robes. He&#39;d get that transcribed, and they&#39;d read the
music back to him, which was made into the scores—a very expensive
process. To stand on 6th Avenue and to compose these rich scores is
extraordinary—keeping all the voices in your mind.”
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RW8SBwGNcF8
His works feature odd but catchy syncopated rhythms, commonly in 5/4,
over which a harmony is typically introduced, played by saxophones
(“Birds Lament”) or sung by a booming men’s choir (“New Amsterdam”).
After years of solitary study, Moondog expertly embedded rules of
counterpoint at the heart of all of his compositions, defined as the
technical foundation of polyphonous sound which consists of combining
multiple melodic layers within one piece. The result is a body of
precisely imagined compositions with flawless instrumentation, strictly
working within classical principles whilst paradoxically having the
impression of being improvised.

With the beat counter-culture and psychadelica of the late 1960s,
Moondog’s song “All Is Loneliness” was covered by Janis Joplin’s group,
Big Brother and the Holding Company. James Guercio, a producer for

Naz Malik
Columbia at the time, brought Moondog into the studio to record his
self-titled 1969 album, drawing attention from a young Phillip Glass.
Along with his friend Steve Reich, Glass invited Moondog to stay at his
place. For a time they lived and played together, sharing techniques and
ideas. A year later Moondog flew to Germany for a concert and didn’t
return for 20 years. He fell in love with the land of the great composers
and a girl who helped him with his music, living simply, happy and
content. No longer seeing him on the streets, many in New York simply
supposed he had passed away.

Moondog finally died in Germany in 1999, but before that had his
recognition. In 1989 he had something of a homecoming show in
Brooklyn, NY, in which old friend Phillip Glass invited him to conduct the
Brooklyn Philharmonic Chamber Orchestra. More recently his music has
been sampled by Mr Scruff and featured in the Big Lebowski, but for
decades it was the simply the sound of that strange old man, dressed as
a Viking on 6 th Avenue.
